# The Pact

> The Pact forbids magic worked on living bodies, and glyphs that change them: petrification above all. Blood ink is
> the Brimhats' ink. And no one but a witch may see how magic is made.
>
> *On Forbidden Magic, Book of Contraptions*

Break the Pact, and sooner or later the Knights Moralis come. The whole story is in *The Black-Bound Book*
(see [Books](books.md#the-black-bound-book)): Brimhats rarely leave one behind, and now and then it turns up
in old chests.

## The Day of the Pact

> Once, anyone with ink and a pen could work magic, and some worked terrible things with it. On the Day of the Pact
> the witches took the knowledge from everyone else and swore to keep it among themselves. Since then the world
> believes magic is a gift one is born with. It is not: it is drawing, and that is the secret every witch keeps.
>
> *The Day of the Pact, The Black-Bound Book*

## What breaks it

| Act | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Petrification | Very heavy |
| Killing a talebearer to keep it quiet | Heavy, and the knights always learn of it |
| A talebearer ringing the village bell | More than the sighting itself |
| Being seen drawing or casting by villagers | Moderate, once per glyph |
| Drawing with blood ink | Light, but every stroke counts |

Your debt to the Pact fades slowly with time.

Earth in a ring of its own under the gaze of four Eyes, with four more Earth seals in lunes cut from the rim (see
[Mixed sigils](glyphs.md#mixed-sigils)). A ball of glowing cyan crystal bursts out of the glyph and grows about a block
every quarter of a second, up to 2.5 blocks plus 2.5 for every block of ring radius (10 at most). It swallows
everything inside: walls, floors, plants, furniture, even the air, and long crystal shards stand out all round its
surface when it is done.

- Every creature inside slows, stiffens and becomes a glowing crystal statue. Players are only held fast.
- Liquids, and blocks that hold things (chests and the like), are left as they are.
- The glyph's own ground and the space just over it are spared.
- Every crystal block remembers what it was. Broken, it drops nothing, and what it was is lost.

The glyph is drawn in blood ink on the last page of *A Witch's Primer* and in *The Black-Bound Book*, and Brimhats
carry it on torn pages, which also turn up in old chests.

## Integration

<div class="card-media wide"><video autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata" poster="../assets/clips/integration.jpg"><source src="../assets/clips/integration.webm" type="video/webm"></video></div>

Only **Integration** (Earth with four reversed Crush signs) drawn on the crystal undoes it: every block goes back to
what it was. Break the crystal first, and nothing can bring that part back. See [Earth spells](spells/earth.md#integration).

Forbidden magic is not undone by its reverse, though. A statue comes back to life only if the Integration is drawn in
**blood ink**. In any other ink it only half wakes: it stays stone and does not move, but its eyes glint
and now and then it makes its sounds.

## Villagers who see magic

A villager who is awake, grown, within 16 blocks, facing you and able to see you notices when you draw or cast. It
takes fright and runs, thinks worse of you (its trades get dearer, and in the end the village golem turns on you),
and the Pact counts it. A whole glyph drawn in front of villagers counts once, not stroke by stroke.

A villager who saw you does not keep quiet for long. It becomes a **talebearer** (angry clouds over its head) and runs
for the village bell. If it rings it, the whole village hears and thinks worse of you, and the Pact counts it again.
After about a minute the tale goes stale and it gives up. Killing a talebearer to keep it quiet is worse still.

Draw where no one is watching: behind walls, or with your back to the village.

## Seal of Obliviation

<div class="card spell"><div class="card-media"><div class="glyph"><img src="../assets/spells/seal_of_obliviation.svg" alt="The Seal of Obliviation glyph"></div></div><div class="recipe"><span class="recipe-label">Inside</span><span class="chip"><img src="../assets/signs/vision.svg" alt="">Vision in its own ring</span><span class="plus">+</span><span class="chip"><img src="../assets/signs/pull.svg" alt="">4 × Pull</span></div></div>

The one spell on a living mind the Pact allows, and only to keep the secret. Close it on a witness before it reaches
the bell, drawn where the witness stands or from a page in your palm while you look at it, and its last minute is gone:
the fright, the bad word about you, the sighting itself. If no other villager carries the same tale, the Pact forgets it
too. Creatures that were after you forget why. It does nothing to players. The seal is in *The Black-Bound Book*;
see [Signs alone](spells/signs-alone.md#seal-of-obliviation).

## The knights' hunt

Once your debt is great enough there is no warning but a low bell and a feeling of being watched. From then on,
now and then, the order notices, and Knights Moralis step out of the air around you.

- The heavier the debt, the more knights come: one, two or three.
- Running does not help. Leave the knights behind for about half a minute (outrun, hidden, or in country they cannot
  reach) and others are sent from where you are now.
- The hunt ends when the debt is paid: when the knights strike you down, or when time has forgiven you enough.
  Dying while you are hunted clears your debt entirely, and the knights go their way.
- **Sinsinger's Talons.** The knights would rather take a witch alive. Bound fast by their pennant's light and worn
  down to half your health, you see the pennant turn black: the seal of obliviation on it takes hold and erases. The
  hunt is over and most of the debt with it, and you wake where you stood, blind for a moment, slowed and dizzy, with
  everything you carried.
- A **Brimhat Mask** fools the Brimhats, and the knights too, into thinking you are one: they attack masked players
  on sight.

Creative and spectator players are never hunted and never seen by villagers.
