# Getting started

Every witch begins the same way: with a pen. This page takes you from nothing to your first working glyph.

## 1. Find a book

<div class="card mini-row"><img class="icon big" src="../assets/icons/grimoire.png" alt=""><div><strong>A Witch's Primer</strong><p>How glyphs work, and three glyphs to start with: Lantern, Birds of Light and Pillar of Flame.</p></div></div>

You need to know what to draw, and glyphs are learned from old books. The easiest first book comes from the
**Stranger**, a silent figure who sometimes visits villages: the first time you speak to them they give you
*A Witch's Primer*, an ink pen and a bottle of conjuring ink. See [Creatures](creatures.md#stranger).

The other glyphs are spread over themed volumes found in library ruins, stronghold libraries and other old chests,
or bought from the Stranger. Each glyph is in exactly one book: see [Books](books.md) for which.

## 2. Make a pen and an inkwell

The pen is a feather, an iron nugget and a stick; the inkwell is a gold nugget over a cup of glass panes. Recipes
for these and the other tools are on [Inks and tools](inks.md#tools).

## 3. Brew ink

<div class="flow"><span class="step"><img class="icon" src="../assets/icons/silverwood_sapling.png" alt=""><span>Silverwood</span></span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="step"><img class="icon" src="../assets/icons/blackened_silverwood_twig.png" alt=""><span>Blackened Twig</span></span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="step"><img class="icon" src="../assets/icons/woodcruor.png" alt=""><span>Woodcruor</span></span><span class="arrow">→</span><span class="step"><img class="icon" src="../assets/icons/conjuring_ink.png" alt=""><span>Conjuring Ink</span></span></div>

Silverwood grows rarely in forests. Breaking its logs now and then drops a **Blackened Silverwood Twig**.
In a brewing stand, a twig in a water bottle gives **Woodcruor**, and woodcruor with glowstone dust gives
**Conjuring Ink**. Pour the bottle into your inkwell. Other inks are on [Inks and tools](inks.md).

## 4. Draw

Hold the pen in one hand and the inkwell in the other (or use the pen on a placed inkwell to dip it). Hold Use on a
surface and move your view: the pen follows where you look. Sneak to rub out.

A glyph has three parts:

1. **The ring** around everything. A bigger ring makes a stronger spell.
2. **The sigil** in the middle: the element.
3. **The signs** around the sigil: what the element does.

Start with the **Lantern**: just the Light sigil inside a ring, no signs.

## 5. Close the ring

Nothing happens until the ring is closed. Witches leave a small gap and close it when they need the spell.
The glyph then works for as long as it stays whole: break its ring, rub it out, or let rain and feet wear it
away, and it stops.

## The Imagined Ring

No hand draws a true circle by itself. Once you have read **Notes on the Imagined Ring**, a thin notebook that is
rare in library chests and sold by the Stranger, you can lay down a perfect ring: go round **twice in the air** over the surface without lifting the pen, and on the third time round
the ring is laid down true, as big as you circled, with the small gap every witch leaves. Lift the pen sooner and
the line goes down exactly as drawn.

## Prepared pages

You do not have to draw in the middle of a fight. Draw a glyph on paper or in a **Palm Quire** with its ring left
open. When you need it, hold the page and press ++v++: the ring closes and the spell fires from the page, where
you look.

## Tracing from a book

Hold a book with a pen in the other hand and the page's glyph shows faintly where you look. Scroll to size it,
sneak to pin it, then trace over it. A faithful copy casts as the page's glyph.
