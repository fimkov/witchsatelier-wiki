# Getting started

Every witch begins the same way: with a pen. This page takes you from nothing to your first working glyph.

## 1. Find a book

You need to know what to draw. Glyphs are learned from **Old Grimoires**:

- Chests in dungeons, mineshafts, desert and jungle temples, woodland mansions, ancient cities, ruined portals
  and trial chamber vaults sometimes hold a grimoire or a few crumpled pages with prepared glyphs.
- Stronghold libraries and **library ruins** (round towers of old stone full of shelves, found in the world)
  hold them far more often, and some have glyphs left drawn on the floor.
- The **Stranger** sometimes visits villages. The first time you speak to them they give you a grimoire,
  an ink pen and a bottle of conjuring ink. After that they trade. See [Creatures](creatures.md#stranger).

Every grimoire opens with two pages: *On Glyphs* (how glyphs work) and *The Imagined Ring*
(a drawing technique, see below). Read both.

## 2. Make a pen and an inkwell

| Item | Recipe |
| --- | --- |
| Ink Pen | Feather (top right), iron nugget (centre), stick (bottom left) |
| Inkwell | Gold nugget on top, glass panes in a cup shape below it |
| Rag | Any wool + string (shapeless) |

A bottle of ink holds 256 blocks of line. An inkwell holds four bottles: click ink bottles onto it in your
inventory, or use them on a placed inkwell.

## 3. Get ink

Ink is brewed in a brewing stand. You need **silverwood**, a tree that grows rarely in forests.
Breaking a silverwood log sometimes drops a **Blackened Silverwood Twig**.

1. Water bottle + blackened silverwood twig = **Woodcruor**
2. Woodcruor + glowstone dust = **Conjuring Ink**

Other inks are brewed from conjuring ink. See [Inks](inks.md).

## 4. Draw

Hold the pen in one hand and the inkwell in the other (or use the pen on a placed inkwell to dip it).
Then hold Use on a surface and move your view: the pen follows where you look. Sneak to rub out, or wipe
lines away with a rag.

A glyph has three parts:

1. **The ring**: a circle around everything. A bigger ring makes a stronger spell.
2. **The sigil** in the middle: the element (fire, water, earth, wind, light and a few others).
3. **The signs** around the sigil: what the element does (rise as a column, spill out, fly as bolts...).

Start with the **Lantern**: just the Light sigil inside a ring, no signs.

## 5. Close the ring

Nothing happens until the ring is closed. Witches leave a small gap in the ring and close it when they need
the spell. The glyph works for as long as it stays whole: break its ring, rub it out, or let rain and
passing feet wear it away, and it stops. A neat hand wears longer.

## The Imagined Ring

No hand draws a true circle by itself. Once you have read *The Imagined Ring* page in a grimoire, you can
lay down a perfect ring: go round **twice in the air** over the surface without lifting the pen, and on the
third time round the ring is laid down true, as big as you circled, with the small gap every witch leaves.
Lift the pen sooner and the line goes down exactly as you drew it.

## Prepared pages

You do not have to draw in the middle of a fight. Draw a glyph on paper or in a **Palm Quire**
(leather, three paper, string) with the ring left open. When you need it, hold the page and press ++v++:
the ring closes and the spell fires from the page, where you look.

- A palm quire holds up to 32 pages. Ink cannot be rubbed out of it.
- Press ++t++ while it is open to tear a page out. Click paper or crumpled pages onto it to put them in.

## Tracing from a grimoire

Hold a grimoire with a pen in the other hand: the page's glyph shows faintly where you look. Scroll to size
it, press ++shift++ to pin it, then trace over it. A faithful copy casts as the page's glyph.
Glyphs do not cast from the grimoire itself; you have to copy them.
