# Glyphs

A glyph is a ring with a **sigil** in its middle and **signs** around it. The sigil is the element;
the signs say what it does. Nothing happens until the ring is closed.

## Rules of drawing

- **Size.** A bigger ring is a stronger spell.
- **Neatness.** A neat hand makes a stronger glyph that wears more slowly.
- **Wear.** A glyph works for as long as it is whole. Rain washes ink away (unless it is waterproof),
  feet scuff it, a rag or a sneaking pen rubs it out.
- **Orientation.** Signs pointing the wrong way do the opposite: Levitation presses down, Pull pushes away,
  Crush mends.
- **Counting.** Some signs count: each Bolt is one fireball, each Bird is one bird.
- **Mixing.** Two kinds of shaping in one ring work side by side, as long as each is drawn at least twice.
  A single stray sign does nothing.
- **Surface.** A glyph drawn on a wall throws its spell sideways. Earth magic needs a surface: when casting
  from a page, look at the block you want it to work on.

## Sigils

| Sigil | Element |
| --- | --- |
| Fire | Flame, heat, fireballs |
| Water | Water, jets, floods |
| Earth | Stone and soil |
| Wind | Air and draughts |
| Light | Light, birds and threads of light |
| Wind Underfoot | The sigil of sylph shoes: leaping and soft falls |
| Airy Forms | Breathing under water |

Some signs can stand alone in the middle of a ring in place of a sigil: **Window**, **Diamond**,
**Vision** and **Dancing Puppet**.

## Signs

| Sign | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Column | The element rises as a pillar or a jet |
| Dispersion | The element spills out all around |
| Levitation | Lifts what stands over the glyph. Reversed, presses down |
| Float | Holds you gently so you sink slowly and land soft |
| Pull | Draws things in. Reversed, pushes them away. Turned along the ring, makes a swirl |
| Crush | Grinds the surface to rubble. Reversed (Integration), puts stone back together |
| Bolt | Fires a projectile; one per sign |
| Direction | Tells the element which way to go: all one way, it goes that way; all out, it fans wide; all in, it draws to a point |
| Convergence | Gathers the spell into a narrow, far-reaching line |
| Radial | Tames fire into warmth: nothing catches, but snow and ice melt |
| Crosshair | Turns the spell towards the nearest creature in front of it, hostile ones first |
| Tread | The glyph wakes but holds its spell until a creature steps on it. Its maker can walk over it safely |
| Collection | Loose items and experience drift in and settle on the glyph |
| Rain | Soft motes fall over the area |
| Bird | A bird of the element flies out; one per sign |
| Weave | Threads bind whatever stands in the spell |
| Bend | Turns sight aside, hiding whoever stands by the glyph |
| Vision | Sight in the dark |
| Diamond | A protective shell. Add it to any glyph to give it a ward |
| Window | Opens a windowway |
| Dancing Puppet | Creatures around forget what they meant to do and dance |
| Eye | A petrifying gaze. Forbidden, see [The Pact](pact.md) |

Band signs, drawn between the two rings of a nested glyph:

| Sign | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Repetition | Keeps setting the glyph back to how it was: rain and feet cannot wear it away |
| Enlarge | Makes the spell inside stronger, one step per sign, up to three |
| Billowing | Everything around falls soft, as on a cloud |

## Nested glyphs

A ring inside a ring shapes the spell within. Draw a whole glyph, then a larger ring around it, and put
signs in the band between the two rings. Any sigil drawn in the band adds its element to the spell inside.
Nothing works until the **outer** ring is closed.

## Linked glyphs

Glyphs joined by a line of ink are one work. Draw a line from the rim of one ring to the rim of another:
when one wakes, every glyph joined to it wakes too, even one whose own ring was left open. Each glyph in the
chain makes all the others stronger, so many small linked glyphs beat one big one.

## Spells

Counts below are what the books draw. Most spells still work with more or fewer signs; the count changes
the strength or, for Bolts and Birds, the number of shots.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Pillar of Flame | Fire + 4 Column | A column of flame rises from the glyph, burning what stands in it. Add a Convergence to make it narrow and long |
| Fire Bolts | Fire + 4 Bolt | A volley of fireballs, one per Bolt, more for a bigger ring. From a page it shoots where you look |
| Sentinel Bolts | Fire + 3 Bolt + 1 Crosshair | A sentry: turns and fires at the nearest creature in front of it |
| Pillar and Volley | Fire + 2 Column + 2 Bolt | A pillar that throws fireballs at the same time |
| Fan of Flame | Fire + 4 Direction, all pointing out | Flame fans out wide |
| Warming Hearth | Fire + 4 Radial | Warmth without flame: melts snow and ice around it |
| Snare of Flame | Fire + 3 Column + 1 Tread | A hidden trap: the pillar bursts up only when a creature steps on it |

## Windowways

Draw a Window glyph on a wall, floor or door, then another somewhere else: they join. A new window joins the
last of yours still waiting. Joined windows share a rim colour; a waiting one stays dark grey. Make the ring at
least two and a half blocks across, or you will not fit through.

## Sylph shoes

Draw **Wind Underfoot** on the soles of any boots: the glyph is split across both soles and closes when the
heels click together. While worn, jump twice to take off and fly as in creative: jump to rise, sneak to sink.
Click your heels again, or touch the ground, to land. Nothing flies under water, and water washes the glyph
off unless the ink is waterproof.

## Contraptions

A glyph drawn onto a thing makes the thing do magic by itself. This is the only way people who are not
witches can use magic at all. Some contraptions hold their seal for good; most wear out and need a witch to
restore them: use a pen with ink on the contraption. See [Artifacts](artifacts.md).
